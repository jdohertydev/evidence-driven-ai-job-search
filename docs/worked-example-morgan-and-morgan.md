# Worked example: Morgan & Morgan Business and Technology

This case study uses my application for the **AI / Automation Engineer** role at Morgan & Morgan Business and Technology as a real example of the workflow.

The vacancy was useful because it matched several career-fit signals that had already been defined before I began the application: practical automation, AI-assisted workflows, process improvement, troubleshooting, testing, documentation, communication and learning unfamiliar technology.

## 1. Requirement extraction

The advert emphasised work such as:

- scoping automation requirements;
- designing and maintaining automated workflows;
- integrating platforms through APIs and webhooks;
- automating repetitive business processes;
- building AI-driven solutions;
- testing prompts and outputs for consistency;
- documenting and handing over completed automations;
- investigating failures and improving reliability;
- learning Microsoft and cross-vendor AI tooling.

It also explicitly valued candidates who build things, even without years of commercial automation experience.

## 2. Evidence mapping

Rather than start by drafting a cover letter, I mapped those requirements to evidence I could actually defend.

| Requirement | Evidence available | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow automation | n8n/OpenAI project; Zapier/VBA calendar automation | Strong |
| AI-assisted workflows | n8n workflow using constrained OpenAI output and human review | Strong |
| Testing and reliability | n8n failure testing; PowerShell dry runs and verification | Strong |
| Scripting | PowerShell, Python and VBA project/work examples | Strong |
| APIs/webhooks | API/webhook work in portfolio projects | Credible supporting evidence |
| Documentation/handover | Project documentation plus historical staff training/support | Strong |
| Client/user communication | Long-standing user support, training and implementation experience | Strong |
| Power Automate / Entra / Intune / Copilot Studio | Limited or no direct hands-on evidence | Development gap |

The important part of the matrix is the last row. The workflow is designed to expose gaps, not make them disappear.

## 3. Project selection

I reviewed my public GitHub projects for recruiter-facing evidence rather than simply choosing the most technically complicated repository.

The strongest primary example was **AI-Assisted Submission-Scope Guidance**, because it demonstrated:

- n8n workflow design;
- OpenAI integration;
- deterministic validation separated from model judgement;
- structured output checking;
- human decision points;
- controlled failure routes;
- testing;
- documentation.

Other projects were useful for different signals: repetitive-process automation, scripting, verification, APIs, webhooks and privacy-aware design.

## 4. Positioning decision

My historic job titles do not immediately read as "AI / Automation Engineer". The evidence underneath them is more relevant than the titles suggest.

The application therefore needed to surface technical and automation evidence **before** conventional career chronology, while still using earlier roles to demonstrate implementation, troubleshooting, training, documentation and process improvement.

The intended narrative became:

> Across different roles and personal projects, I have repeatedly identified inefficient or broken processes, researched solutions and constraints, learned unfamiliar technology, built practical systems, tested and troubleshot them, documented them and helped other people use them. Formal full-stack development training and recent AI/automation projects have developed that pattern further.

## 5. Drafting and critique

The cover letter was not treated as a one-pass generation task.

I repeatedly challenged drafts against questions such as:

- Does this actually read like a cover letter rather than a project summary?
- Does the opening establish motivation before technical detail?
- Is the evidence specific enough?
- Is any wording generic recruitment language?
- Does the draft acknowledge genuine gaps without weakening the overall case?
- Could I defend every technical claim in an interview?

When a draft sounded polished but felt structurally wrong, it was rejected and reworked.

## 6. Human control

AI assisted with research synthesis, comparison, drafting, critique and evidence checking. I retained responsibility for:

- deciding whether the role was genuinely suitable;
- deciding which projects best represented my ability;
- judging whether wording sounded like me;
- rejecting claims that exceeded the evidence;
- deciding what should appear in the final application.

## Why this example matters

The case study is intentionally a little self-referential: the employer I was applying to asked candidates to show what they had built, and the process of identifying and developing the application itself had become a structured AI-assisted workflow.

I do not present that as a substitute for my technical portfolio. It is evidence of a different thing: how I approach a messy, ambiguous problem involving research, constraints, evidence, AI assistance and human judgement.
