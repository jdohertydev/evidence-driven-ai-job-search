# Job-fit screening

The discovery problem is not simply finding vacancies containing a particular keyword. Job titles are inconsistent, and a role outside an obvious career path can still be highly relevant.

The screening workflow therefore uses **career-fit signals** rather than a single title or keyword match.

## Positive signals

Examples include:

- applied AI systems;
- workflow or process improvement;
- automation;
- digital products;
- educational technology;
- AI enablement or adoption;
- operational improvement;
- practical problem-solving with technology;
- troubleshooting;
- implementation;
- building or improving systems;
- turning ideas into useful working processes;
- meaningful autonomy and real responsibility.

## Negative signals

Examples include roles dominated by:

- routine administration;
- generic repetitive customer service;
- aggressive sales;
- pure classroom teaching;
- heavy supervision;
- little connection to systems improvement or technology.

These are **ranking signals, not hard filters**. A vacancy can still be surfaced when the overall pattern is interesting.

## Current workflow behaviour

The scheduled job-alert review:

1. checks for new Indeed alert emails;
2. reviews the vacancies contained in those alerts;
3. assesses each role against the fit criteria;
4. surfaces only strong or credible possible matches;
5. avoids repeating roles already reported;
6. includes uncertainties where the alert contains limited information;
7. leaves the decision to investigate or apply with me.

## Why this matters

The purpose is not to maximise the number of applications. It is to reduce low-value manual checking while preserving unusual opportunities that a crude keyword filter might miss.

The Morgan & Morgan AI / Automation Engineer vacancy became a useful worked example because it strongly matched several pre-existing signals at once: automation, AI, process improvement, troubleshooting, implementation, testing, documentation and learning unfamiliar technology.
